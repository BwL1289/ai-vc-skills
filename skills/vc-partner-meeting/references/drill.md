# The drill

The vc-partner-meeting skill's live drill, run at its step 6. The skill's own step 6 links here and keeps the rules that
bind every part of it; the skill's test checks both. The rules on drafts, follow-ups and a changed figure came from the
skill's first live drill and are this skill's own, unverified.

- **The first turn** names the lenses and puts question 1; the numbered answer in the skill's Answering section comes
  once the drill ends or the founder stops, so the kept list is not shown before the drill.
- **The founder's own words first.** A question box may offer a drafted answer to take in one click, beside typing one's
  own or pausing; with that choice on every question, a founder may rehearse few answers in their own words. So the
  first three questions, and every one in the ends-the-meeting tier, ask for the founder's own answer with no draft
  shown and no draft to pick, whatever the box offers on the others. Once the founder has answered, or skipped it, the
  draft may be shown beside the answer. Every other question may offer the draft from the start. When the founder asks
  for a draft on every question, the rule still holds on these: say in one line that the draft comes right after their
  answer or a skip. A draft is built as a stronger answer is: only from the founder's material and tool results, every
  gap a bracketed ask, no figure worked out by hand. A draft the founder takes is the skill's text, not the founder's
  answer: it adds nothing to the founder's material, and the debrief says the draft was taken, never that the answer
  held up.
- **A follow-up.** Ask at most one follow-up per question, only when the answer leaves out the specific thing the
  question asked for, and quote the part of the answer it follows. Word it in the founder's everyday words, never this
  skill's terms (a lens, a tier, a kept question) or investors' jargon (an "ICP", a "wedge"): a plain question, then
  what is enough to answer it, such as "Which kind of customer paid first, and when? The type of customer and rough
  timing is enough." A follow-up is the moderator's own question, put to the founder directly; it is never worded as a
  partner's.
- **A figure that changes.** When the founder changes a figure mid-session (a forecast revised, a count corrected),
  label the new figure like any answer and mark every draft that cites the old one, in the drill, the standing prep or
  a stronger answer, "stale" followed by the figure's name in the founder's words, then redo each from the new figure.
  A figure a tool computed goes through that tool again, a new call with the new figure as step 3 says, never an edit
  of the old result or the new figure worked out by hand. A figure has changed only when the founder says the new one
  replaces one they gave; an answer whose figure differs from the material's with no such word is a disagreement for
  the debrief, and a bracketed ask asks which stands before anything is redone. The old line stays quoted as written
  and is no longer a disagreement; bringing it up to date is one open ask. For the changed figure the new call replaces
  the old one: its verdict, mismatches and flags are the ones the drill's order, the kept table and the answer's Numbers
  use.
