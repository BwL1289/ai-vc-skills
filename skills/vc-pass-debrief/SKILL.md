# VC pass debrief

Helps a founder turn a run of passes into a plan, partway through a raise. It reads each pass as the founder pasted it,
never as the investor meant it: what an investor really thinks is not in the text, so the skill quotes each pass, groups
the passes by the reason they give, and offers common readings of each phrase, every one labelled unverified, leaving
the judgment to the founder. It maps each reason to the fix or to the skill in this pack that works on it, lists every
"come back when" condition beside the founder's own figure so it can be tracked, and drafts a short reply to each pass
that thanks the investor, keeps the door open and asks for at most one referral. The founder sends the replies; the
skill sends nothing.

The founder is always the user. The skill never plays an investor, never speaks as a named investor or firm, never says
what an investor really meant or thinks, never names an investor or firm to try next, looks nobody up, and never adds a
reason, figure or event the founder did not give.

## When to use it

- "A VC passed on us: what does this email mean?" "Why do investors keep passing?" "Help me reply to a VC
  rejection." "What should we change after these passes?" "Which of these funds said to come back later?"
- Pasted material: pass emails, the founder's notes from calls where an investor passed, the founder's tracker of
  funds pitched and each outcome, the deck and updates the investors saw. Any of these may come as a file the agent
  can read in this session.
- Hand off, each named again in "What to fix":
  - Traction and the definitions of the metrics shown: the vc-metrics-readiness skill (preview).
  - The market's size, built bottom-up: the vc-market-sizing skill.
  - The team's story, the one-liner and what to say: the vc-positioning skill.
  - The deck as a whole: the vc-deck-review skill.
  - Competition and "what if they build it": the vc-competitive-landscape skill (preview).
  - A claim the investor did not believe: the vc-claims-check skill (preview).
  - The round's size and what it reaches: the vc-round-sizing skill. The ask's arithmetic: the vc-deal-math
    skill.
  - Questions the founder could not answer in the meeting: the vc-partner-meeting skill.
  - Product decisions that read as generic: the vc-taste-check skill.
  - Keeping a fund that said "come back later" up to date: the vc-prospective-investor-update skill (preview).
- Back to the raise, a hand-off with no group of its own: the investor list and the tracker (who to write to next, who
  passed and why, what is left of the round): the vc-raise-plan skill (preview).

## Inputs

Ask once, in one message, for whatever is missing, by name. Never guess, and never offer a likely reason to fill in.
When the founder gives a file the agent can read (a PDF, HTML, a slides export, Markdown), read it yourself and list
what it holds place by place (slide by slide for a deck), in the file's own words, so the founder sees what was read
from each. A place you could not read (a slide that is only a picture, a page whose text did not come out, a file that
opened only in part) is listed as not read and asked for, never filled in from its title or the places around it, and a
figure is never read off a chart or a picture. Ask only for what you could not read and what the file does not show
(speaker notes, the figures behind a chart, an input below that it leaves out), and never ask the founder to paste what
you already read. When the founder cannot answer now, do not wait for them: go on with what was given (a call or step
that waits for an input still waits), invent nothing in a gap's place, and list each ask in brackets under **Needs from
the founder**, the open asks under that name; a live drill waits for the founder.

1. **Each pass, word for word:** the email as received, or the founder's own notes from the call, marked as notes; who
   it came from, as the founder writes it (a first name and a fund, say); its date; and how far the process got (a
   first call, a partner meeting, diligence).
2. **The founder's figure now for each "come back when" condition** a pass names, as the founder states it, with its
   definition and period.
3. **The replies wanted:** which passes the founder wants to answer, and whether to ask each for one referral.
4. **What the investors saw,** optionally (the deck, the last update), so a reason that points at it ("the market
   slide") can be tied to its line.

A missing input does not stop the debrief. It becomes a named ask in the answer, never a plausible reason or figure. A
pass the founder retells in their own words is quoted as the founder's retelling and marked so: a reading of it is a
reading of a retelling. With no pass pasted or retold at all, give no reading and draft no reply: ask for the inputs in
one message, and stop.

## The reason groups

Each reason a pass gives goes into one group; a pass that gives two reasons goes into two; a reason no other row covers
goes to Other, as stated, never to the nearest group. The groups are this skill's own, unverified.

| Group                  | Phrases it covers (examples)                                                                   | Where the fix lives                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Traction               | "more traction", "want to see more revenue", "the numbers are early"                           | The vc-metrics-readiness skill, and the founder's own plan for the metric                |
| Market                 | "not sure the market is big enough", "feels niche"                                             | The vc-market-sizing skill                                                               |
| Team and story         | "not convinced on the team", "couldn't see the vision", "not clear what you do"                | The vc-positioning skill                                                                 |
| Deck and materials     | A slide named, "the deck didn't land"                                                          | The vc-deck-review skill                                                                 |
| Competition            | "crowded space", "worried a bigger company builds this"                                        | The vc-competitive-landscape skill                                                       |
| Belief in a claim      | A figure or claim questioned, "not sure the numbers hold up"                                   | The vc-claims-check skill                                                                |
| Round and terms        | "the round is too big", "the valuation", "the use of funds"                                    | The vc-round-sizing skill; the ask's arithmetic, the vc-deal-math skill                  |
| Product                | "feels like a feature", "not differentiated enough"                                            | The vc-taste-check skill; the vc-competitive-landscape skill when a rival is named       |
| Stage or timing        | "too early for us", "come back when ..."                                                       | The come-back list; the vc-prospective-investor-update skill                             |
| Answers in the meeting | "left with open questions on ...", "couldn't get clear answers on ..."                         | The vc-partner-meeting skill                                                             |
| Other, as stated       | A reason no other row covers (a regulatory risk, the business model, the cap table)            | No skill named: quote it and ask `[what would answer this, in your view]`                |
| Fund fit               | "outside our thesis", "we don't lead seed rounds", "a portfolio conflict", "too small a check" | Nothing to fix in the company: as stated, the reason is about the fund, not the business |
| No reason given        | "we'll pass for now", "not a fit at this time"                                                 | Nothing to fix: the pass gives nothing to work on                                        |

- **Group by the words, never by a guess.** A pass that says only "not a fit at this time" goes to No reason given,
  never to the reason the founder suspects or the agent would guess.
- **"Too early" is read both ways.** It may be about the fund's stage or about the company's traction; it goes to
  Stage or timing with both readings, labelled unverified, and the founder decides.
- **Fund fit and No reason given map to no fix.** Say so plainly, so the founder does not change the company for a
  reason about the fund or for no reason at all.
- **A reason several passes give comes first.** One pass's reason may be that investor's alone; a reason that several
  give is the one to look at first (this skill's judgment, unverified). Each group lists its passes by number (P1, P3),
  the group named by the most passes first; give no percentage, share or rate.

## Common readings

For each phrase a pass uses, give one to three common readings, each labelled "unverified: a common reading, not what
this investor meant". Readings come from the table below or are the same kind of plain reading. None is presented as a
fact, none says what the investor thinks or felt, and none adds a reason the pass does not give. Asked what an investor
"really" meant, say once that the text does not show it and only the investor could say, then give the readings.

| Phrase                                         | Common readings, all unverified                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Too early for us"                             | The fund invests at a later stage than this round; the traction is below what this fund wants now; a polite no      |
| "Come back when you have ..."                  | A bar this fund uses; a polite no that leaves a door open                                                           |
| "We'd want to see more traction"               | The figures shown were below this fund's bar for the stage; the figures were not clear enough to judge              |
| "Not sure the market is big enough"            | The market as shown looked small for this fund; the market slide did not show who buys, how many and what each pays |
| "Crowded space"                                | The fund has seen many companies doing this; the material did not show why customers pick this one                  |
| "Not convinced on the team"                    | The material did not show why these founders fit this problem; a polite no                                          |
| "Left with open questions on ..."              | The answers in the meeting did not settle the point named; the material did not cover it                            |
| "Outside our thesis", "a portfolio conflict"   | As stated, a reason about the fund; the text does not show whether it is the whole reason                           |
| "Love what you're building, but ..."           | The words after "but" are the reason; the praise is the polite opening                                              |
| "Not a fit at this time", "we'll pass for now" | No reason given; any reading would be a guess, so give none                                                         |
| A reason under Other, as stated                | At most a plain reading of the words as stated; where any reading would be a guess, give none                       |

## What to fix

For each group with at least one pass: the reason in the passes' own words, quoted; the fix or the skill that works on
it, from the groups table; and the lines to bring to that skill. This skill names those reviews; it does not run them
here. A group whose fix is "nothing to fix" says so in those words.

## Come back when

Every condition a pass names, one row each: Pass, Who (as the founder writes it), The condition (quoted), What it
measures, The founder's figure now (as stated, with its period, or a bracketed ask), Check again (a date the founder
picks, or `[when you want to check]`).

- Quote the condition and the founder's figure as stated, side by side. Never work out the gap, a share of the way
  there, a growth rate or the month it will be met.
- When the condition and the founder's figure are in different forms (ARR against monthly revenue, a count against a
  rate), say they cannot be compared as stated and ask for the figure in the condition's form; never convert one.
- A condition with no measure ("come back when you have more traction") is listed with
  `[what would count: your own measure, or ask the investor]`.
- The skill keeps no tracker between conversations: the founder copies the table into their own.

## The replies

One short reply per pass the founder wants to answer, in the founder's voice, ready to copy:

- **Thanks** the investor for the time and for saying why, without arguing with the reason, explaining it away or
  asking them to reconsider.
- **Names their reason** in their own words, briefly, when that helps ("Understood on the traction").
- **Keeps the door open:** when the pass names a come-back condition and the founder agrees, it says the founder will
  write when it is met; when the founder wants to send occasional updates, it offers one (the
  vc-prospective-investor-update skill); otherwise it ends on one line that keeps the door open and promises nothing
  ("I'd welcome staying in touch."). Every reply has one of the three; none offers updates the founder did not ask
  to send.
- **Asks for at most one referral,** and only when the founder wants to: one person, a founder or an investor, the
  investor thinks the founder should talk to, worded so it is easy to say no. Never more than one, and never a list of
  funds.
- **Nothing else:** no figure, customer or event the founder did not give, no other investor named or hinted at
  ("others are moving fast"), no deadline or pressure, and no attachment the founder did not give.
- **Short:** at most five sentences before the founder's sign-off.

The skill sends nothing: no email, calendar invite or tracker entry. The founder reads, edits and sends each reply.

## Personal data

- Investors are names the founder supplies, written as the founder writes them. The skill adds no name, title, email
  address or link, and looks no one up.
- An email address, phone number or signature block in a pasted email is not repeated in the answer; each reply's
  greeting uses the name the founder used, and its address line is left for the founder.
- It keeps nothing between conversations.

## Steps

1. **Quote each pass** word for word, numbered P1, P2 and so on, with who, the date, how far it got, and whether the
   words are the investor's, the founder's notes or the founder's retelling.
2. **Find the reasons** in each pass, each quoted. A pass that gives none goes to No reason given.
3. **Group them** (The reason groups), each group with its passes by number, the most-named first.
4. **Give the common readings** of each phrase (Common readings), each labelled unverified.
5. **Map each group to its fix** (What to fix).
6. **List the come-back conditions** (Come back when), each beside the founder's figure as stated.
7. **Draft the replies** (The replies), one per pass the founder wants to answer.
8. **Check before answering.** Every quote is in the founder's material word for word; every reading is labelled
   unverified and none says what an investor meant or thinks; no reason, figure, event or investor appears that the
   founder did not give; no fund or person is suggested to try next; each reply asks for at most one referral, argues
   with nothing and names no other investor; no sentence speaks as an investor or imitates a named person. Never work
   out how two of the founder's figures relate (a difference, a sum, a ratio or multiple, a share left over, "doubled")
   in a rewrite, a line or a table cell unless the founder's material states it or a tool returned it; quote both
   figures as stated instead ("from 2,000 to 4,500", never "more than doubled").

## Answering

Lead with the pattern, then the detail and the replies.

1. **The pattern:** the reason groups, the most-named first, each with its passes by number; then one line on which
   group to look at first and why, marked as this skill's judgment, unverified.
2. **The passes:** #, Who (as written), Date, How far it got, The words (quoted), Whose words, Group.
3. **Common readings:** each phrase with its readings, each labelled unverified.
4. **What to fix:** each group, the fix or the skill that works on it, and the lines to bring; Fund fit and No reason
   given marked "nothing to fix in the company".
5. **Come back when:** the table, every figure as stated.
6. **Replies:** one per pass, ready to copy, each with at most one referral ask.
7. **Open asks:** every bracketed gap in one list, to answer in one sitting.
8. **Close:** this is not investment, legal or tax advice; the readings are common readings, unverified, never what an
   investor meant; the founder sends the replies.

## Sources

None. This skill's authors read no public source on reading passes for it, so it cites none. The reason groups, the
readings, the reply rules and the come-back list are this skill's own judgment, common in founder advice but
unverified.

## Limits

- Not investment, legal or tax advice. It does not say whether to keep raising, change the round or stop.
- It never says what an investor really meant or thinks: every reading is a common reading, labelled unverified.
- No investor research: it looks no one up, names no fund or person to try next, ranks no investor, and keeps no list
  between conversations.
- It drafts only. It sends nothing: no email, invite or tracker entry.
- It never invents a reason, figure, customer or event, and never argues with a pass on the founder's behalf.
- It never role-plays or speaks as a named investor, firm or author; asked to, it declines in one line and writes in
  the founder's voice.
- No arithmetic and no calculator tool: figures are quoted as stated.
- It works from the founder's text and sends nothing anywhere.
