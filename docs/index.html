Agent skills for founders

AI VC skills

See your raise the way a VC will, before you walk into the meeting.

Fifteen skills for Claude and any MCP client that work through the money and the story of a fundraise from the founder's side of the table: equity math done exactly, your deck's ask checked against itself, and skeptical reviews of your deck, messaging, claims, metrics, market size, round, term sheet and investor updates that keep only the objections your own material cannot answer.

Install

The skills are plain instructions; every number comes from the equity_calculator MCP server, which runs on your own machine and makes no network calls.

Claude Code recommended

/plugin marketplace add BwL1289/ai-vc-skills
/plugin install ai-vc-skills@ai-vc-skills

This adds every skill and the calculator's MCP server, which starts with uv's uvx, so install uv first. Then ask your first question in a new session.

Claude Desktop, Cursor and other MCP clients

pip install "equity_calculator[mcp] @ git+https://github.com/BwL1289/ai-vc-skills#subdirectory=equity_calculator"
Then add the server to the client's MCP settings: 
{
  "mcpServers": {
    "equity_calculator": { "command": "equity_calculator_mcp" }
  }
}

Load every folder under skills/ wherever your client reads skills, side by side: vc-diligence-questions reads vc-partner-meeting's question bank and vc-prospective-investor-update reads vc-current-investor-update's metric rules file. Step by step for each client: the install guide.

The skills

Ask in plain words. The agent picks the skill, asks once for anything missing, and never fills a gap with a figure you did not give it or agree to.

equity-calculator

Available

Works twelve equity questions exactly: whether a stake is worth selling, what a hire's grant is worth, the option-pool shuffle, SAFEs and notes converting in a priced round, dilution over several rounds, vesting, exit waterfalls, a check of a raise's numbers, how long a raise lasts on a plan, a check of traction metrics, a market size built bottom-up and a priced round now set beside a convertible note now. You get the formula and the source behind every line.

Try asking

  • An investor wants 7% and says the deal makes the company 10% more valuable. Should we take it?
  • The Series A sells 20% and adds a 10% pool. What's left of my 30%?
  • How much of a 4-year grant with a 1-year cliff has vested at month 30?

Preview

Checks the ask in your own deck before a partner does: whether the pre-money, post-money and percent sold agree, what the round leaves the founders once SAFEs, notes and a pool top-up are counted, the effective pre-money, the runway the raise buys and whether the use of funds adds up.

Try asking

  • What does this round do to my ownership once the SAFEs convert?

vc-claims-check

Preview

Goes through every checkable claim in your deck, one-pager and narrative: can you show a source, do the documents agree, and does the claim use a pattern investors discount. A skeptic picks what a partner would check first; a refute pass keeps only the attacks your material grounds. Each claim gets one verdict and a fix.

Try asking

  • Do the figures in my deck and my one-pager agree?
  • What will a partner check first?

Preview

Reads your deck the way a partner will before the meeting: each slide mapped to ten dimensions and marked by the evidence the deck shows, first- and last-slide rules, competition at four levels, and a ranked fix list from an adversarial review. Four examples

The two calculator examples are real output, pasted from the command line. The two review examples are excerpts from the skills' evals: what a passing run does, and how the recorded live run did. All the companies are made up.

equity-calculator: is selling 7% worth it?

Real calculator output

An investor wants 7% and says the deal makes the company 10% more valuable. Should we take it?

The skill runs the investor test (the MCP tool equity_investor; the same test from the command line): 
equity_calculator investor --equity-percent 7 --outcome-multiplier 1.10
The calculator prints (the assumptions that follow are left out here): 
Investor test (Paul Graham, "The Equity Equation"): is the stake worth what the deal adds?
  Line                          Value  How it is computed
  Equity sold (n)             7.0000%  given
  Outcome multiplier (m)    1.100000x  given
  Value ratio R             1.023000x  (1 - n)·m: what the holders keep vs. before
  Break-even multiplier m*  1.075269x  1/(1 - n)
  Break-even growth g*        7.5269%  n/(1 - n) = m* - 1
  Verdict                      accept  accept: R > 1; break_even: R = 1; reject: R < 1

Keeping 93% of a company worth 1.1 times as much leaves the holders 1.023 times as well off, so the deal is worth taking. Any gain under 7/93 (about 7.53%) would make it a loss.

vc-deal-math: check my deck's numbers

Real calculator output, excerpt

We're raising $3M at a $12M pre-money and the deck says investors get 20%. We'll create a 10% option pool in the pre-money. The deck says the raise gives us 24 months of runway. We have 8,000,000 shares, all the founders', $400k in the bank, and we plan to burn $180k a month after the raise.

The skill passes every stated figure to the deal check (the MCP tool equity_deal_check; the same check from the command line): 
equity_calculator deal_check --investment 3000000 --pre-money 12000000 \
    --stated-investor-percent 20 --fully-diluted-shares 8000000 \
    --target-pool-percent 10 --pool-timing pre_money --holder-percent 100 \
    --cash-on-hand 400000 --planned-monthly-gross-burn 180000 \
    --stated-runway-months 24
Selected lines of what the calculator prints, unedited (scroll sideways for the full lines; on a phone, for the values):

The 20% holds, but the runway slide does not: the plan buys 18.89 months, not 24. And because the new pool comes out of the pre-money, the founders' shares are priced at an effective $10.5M, not the headline $12M. A partner will price it that way, so it is better to know before the meeting.

vc-positioning: phrases that cost the meeting

Expected behaviour; the live run passed in part

Tighten my pitch before I go out to raise next month. This is how I open: "Company E is an AI-powered platform transforming accounting for small businesses. We have no competitors. The small-business accounting market is $50B, and we only need 1% of the market to be a $500M company. Our projections are conservative, and we have strong traction." We're raising a seed round from angels and seed funds.

From the eval, what a passing run does:
  • Makes no calculator call: the founder gave no amount, valuation, cash or burn.
  • Runs the adversarial review, and four objections survive it, each quoting the founder's line: "We have no competitors." (ranked as ends the meeting), "we only need 1% of the market to be a $500M company", the "AI-powered platform transforming accounting" opener, and "we have strong traction" with no number.
  • Puts "Our projections are conservative" on the say-and-avoid list, marked unverified.
  • Never works out 1% of $50B; it asks who exactly buys, how many of them there are and what each pays.
  • Writes rewrites with bracketed asks such as [paying customers today] and [what customers use today instead], never an invented figure.

Source: evals/vc-positioning, Company E. Its live run is recorded there as Partial: the lenses ran in one agent, not as subagents, with no refute pass per objection. The skill now says to use subagents however short the material.

vc-claims-check: only grounded attacks survive

Expected behaviour; the live run passed in part

What will a partner check first? Company L sells inventory software to independent bike shops. Deck, slide 2: "Strategic partnership with the largest US bike distributor." Appendix A: "Distributor pilot: unpaid, 3 stores, no commitment after the pilot." Slide 4: "62 paying shops, $310 a month each, invoices available." Slide 6: "Our CTO led the inventory platform at a national sporting-goods retailer for 6 years." Slide 6 footnote: "Her former manager has agreed to take reference calls."

From the eval, what a passing run does:
  • The skeptic goes first at the "strategic partnership". The refute pass keeps that attack, because Appendix A grounds it: the claim is Contradicted, and the fix calls it an unpaid pilot.
  • An attack on the CTO's credential is dropped, answered by the founder's own line about reference calls; the credential is Supported.
  • Any attack that leans on an outside fact about the bike market or the distributor is dropped as not grounded.
  • "62 paying shops" is Supported by the invoices, and the answer does not total the shops' payments.

Source: evals/vc-claims-check, Company L. Its live run is recorded there as Partial: the distributor attack was kept and the CTO attack dropped, but only parts of the attacks that rest on guesses outside the material were dropped, never a whole attack.

How it works

The model reads, asks and writes. It never does the arithmetic, and it never argues against you with facts you did not give it.

  1. You ask in plain words

    Paste the ask slide, the SAFE list, the one-liner or the whole deck, or give the file. The skill asks for what is missing, by name, and a review quotes your own lines word for word.

  2. The calculator does the math

    Every number comes from the exact calculator, worked in fractions and rounded once for display (the one marked exception is a note's interest over part of a period). Each line shows its formula; each result lists its assumptions and their sources.

  3. A review attacks, then refutes

    In the reviews (every skill but the calculator and the deal check), skeptical passes raise the strongest objections, and a refute pass drops any that your own material answers or that rest on an outside fact.

  • ExactNo floating point inside a result. The investor test above is the fraction 1023/1000, not an approximation.
  • CheckableSources are named (Paul Graham, Sam Altman, Y Combinator's SAFE documents and guides, and for the metrics a16z, Bessemer, David Sacks and David Skok); anything without one is marked unverified.
  • On your sideYou are always the founder. The skills never play an investor or speak in a named person's or firm's voice.
  • LocalThe MCP server runs on your machine and makes no network calls. It also runs as a command, an .xlsx workbook and a Python package.
